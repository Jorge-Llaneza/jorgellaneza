1. Voltage, current, resistance

Voltage (V) is the “push” on the charges, like water pressure from a tank. Current (I) is how much charge flows per second, like the water flow. Resistance (R) is the friction, like a narrow pipe.

They are linked by Ohm’s law: V = I·R. Voltage is applied across things; current flows through them. Try it in “Circuits”.

2. Sources and the sign convention

A voltage source keeps its voltage whatever the current. A current source keeps its current whatever the voltage. If the current leaves the + terminal, the element delivers energy (source); if it enters the +, it absorbs it (load). Resistors always absorb.

3. Series and parallel

Series: same current, resistances add. Parallel: same voltage, conductances add, so the total is always smaller than the smallest resistor. Trick: in series the biggest resistor dominates; in parallel the smallest does.

4. Kirchhoff’s laws

KCL: currents entering a node = currents leaving it. KVL: voltages around any closed loop add up to zero. Write the sign you meet first on each element as you walk the loop. A negative result just means the real current goes the other way.

5. Thevenin: the big trick

Any linear circuit seen from two terminals A–B equals one source VTH in series with one resistor RTH. Steps: open the load → VTH = open-circuit voltage → “kill” sources (voltage source → wire, current source → open) → RTH = resistance seen from A–B. Then changing the load is trivial.

6. Diodes and the Q point

A diode is a one-way valve. Ideal: ON (0 V) when current flows anode→cathode, OFF (no current) otherwise. Real silicon: needs ≈0.7 V to turn on. Zener: also conducts backwards once the reverse voltage reaches VZ. The Q point (operation point) is where the diode’s curve meets the circuit’s load line. Practice in the “Diode” tab.

7. Transistors: electric switches and amplifiers

MOSFET: voltage VGS on the gate (no gate current) opens a channel between drain and source. Below the threshold: cutoff (open switch). Above: ohmic (resistor, a closed switch) or saturation (current source). BJT: a small base current IB controls a big collector current: IC = β·IB. Try both in the next tabs.

8. Capacitors

A capacitor stores energy in an electric field. It blocks DC when full and its voltage can’t jump. With a resistor it charges exponentially with time constant τ = R·C: 63% after 1τ, 99% after 5τ.
